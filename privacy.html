Privacy policy

Last updated: October 6, 2026

This policy explains how the Random Frame application for Windows, Linux, and Android handles data.

1. Data collection

Random Frame has no account, analytics or telemetry. Sync is optional and off until you enable it.

The application does not:

  • create user accounts,
  • use analytics,
  • display advertising,
  • collect telemetry,
  • profile users.

2. Local application data

Random Frame stores a small amount of application state locally on your device for normal application functionality.

This may include:

  • the selected theme (System, Light, or Dark),
  • acknowledgement of the entry notice,
  • browsing history and favorites, with small preview images of those frames,
  • viewing statistics and Seen IDs (identifiers of frames you have viewed, kept to skip repeats),
  • Prnt.sc IDs checked (including ones with no image),
  • if you enable Sync: a pairing ID, a random device ID, the name you give this device, and the time of the last sync on this device, and your recovery key in your system’s secure storage. On Android, the key is encrypted with a key held by Android Keystore.

On Android, this data is left out of device backups and transfers to a new phone, so uninstalling the app deletes it. Sync is the way to keep it across devices.

If you enable Sync, Seen IDs, history, favorites, Prnt.sc IDs checked, Activity Stats, and preferences (theme and history page size) are sent in encrypted form to the Sync server. The list of devices known from past syncs (device name, platform, first joined and last synced times) is also part of the encrypted data. Drawing frames and browsing history still work without Sync.

You can clear history and statistics in the app. Favorites and Seen IDs are kept. With Sync on, linked devices remove known history and Activity operations when they next sync. Unknown offline changes can appear later.

3. Optional Sync

Sync transfers an encrypted copy of Seen IDs, history, favorites, exploration progress, Activity Stats, and preferences. Encryption and decryption happen on your device. The server operator does not receive the plaintext list of identifiers or your recovery key. The server can see connection metadata, the sync ID, and the size and timing of transfers. The list of devices known from past syncs (name, platform, first joined and last synced times) is part of the encrypted copy. Keep your recovery key safe: anyone with it can connect to your Sync. You can show it again from the Sync dialog.

With Sync on, the app contacts the Sync server when it starts and whenever you choose Sync now. On Android, it also syncs when you return to the app at least 15 minutes after the last sync.

4. External services

The Windows and Linux app checks GitHub for updates when it starts. GitHub may receive connection information such as your IP address and request details. Checking does not send your history, favorites, or Seen IDs. The Android app does not check for updates.

Random Frame connects directly from your device to external image and content providers in order to retrieve content.

As part of a normal network connection, those providers may receive technical information such as your IP address, request details and device or network information. Random Frame does not receive or store this information.

Data processed by external providers is subject to their respective privacy policies.

5. Saved files

Random Frame saves files only when you explicitly choose to do so and only to a location selected by you.
